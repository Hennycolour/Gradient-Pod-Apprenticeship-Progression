An apprenticeship worth proving

Tailoring in Lagos is taught the way it has always been taught — beside a master, on real garments, for years. What has been missing is the record.

An apprentice may spend five years in a workshop and leave with nothing a future employer, a bank or a customer can read. The knowledge is real. The evidence is not.

What we changed

The programme breaks the trade into six stages and thirty-one skills. An apprentice logs the work as they do it. A master confirms each skill before it enters the record. Once confirmed, an entry cannot be edited — only added to — so the record is worth something precisely because it is hard to change.

Why a master must sign

Self-reported skill is not evidence. Every confirmed entry carries the name of the master who watched the work and the date they signed it off. That is the difference between a diary and a record.

Who it belongs to

The apprentice. If they move workshop, city or trade, the record moves with them. Workshops can read it; only the apprentice and their confirming master can add to it.
